Briefly explain the difference between staccato, tenuto, and an accent.

nella-fantasia · None

Frozen reference

development-e10

llm · not_applicable · factual 2/4 · 11.14s

General explanation: Staccato, tenuto, and accent are articulation marks that modify how notes are played. Staccato (a dot above or below a note) indicates short, detached notes, played briefly and separated from neighboring notes. Tenuto (a short horizontal line through the note stem) signifies that the note should be sustained for its full written value, sometimes with added emphasis or slight flexibility in timing. Accent (a small triangle above or below the note) highlights the attack of a note, making it stronger or more pronounced without altering its duration. These markings guide performers on articulation and expression but do not change the note's written rhythmic value unless specified otherwise.

The basic meanings of staccato, tenuto and accent are correct, but two symbols are described wrongly. A tenuto is a dash placed above or below the notehead, not through the stem, and a standard accent is a '>' wedge rather than 'a small triangle'.
